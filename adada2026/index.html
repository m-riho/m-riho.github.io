Extending Generative AI-Assisted Algorithmic Art Education Across Academic Disciplines: A Preliminary Study

Kunio KONDO · Tokyo University of Technology

01 / Classroom study

About This Study

Students chose an image they wanted to make and described how to draw it in a natural-language prompt. A generative AI system wrote the program. Students ran it in p5.js, inspected the image, and revised the prompt when the result differed from their intent.

Earlier work introduced this exercise to art and design students and then to law students without requiring prior programming experience. In June 2026, we examined how students from four disciplines experienced the exercise in one university-wide course. After excluding one unevaluable report, we analyzed 170 students.

What did students report?

Students generally evaluated the activity positively across all four disciplines. No disciplinary comparison remained statistically significant after Holm correction. With only 11 science students, comparisons involving that group are particularly uncertain.

Self-reported learning experiences had negligible or weak associations with scores for documentation, reflection, and revision. None of the six correlations remained significant after correction. Students’ perceptions and assessed work may capture different outcomes. This preliminary study does not establish equivalent learning across disciplines or a causal effect of the exercise.
